🎯 What Your Research Must Explain

  • Background: Who are the rivals? When and where did they compete? What were they trying to achieve?
  • Key events: What important events, achievements, or conflicts shaped the rivalry?
  • Effects: What improved? What problems or pressure did the rivalry create? Explain the effects on the rivals and their field.
  • Statements: What did the rivals say about each other or the competition? Show their words and report their meaning.

💬 Useful Language: Direct and Reported Speech

Direct speech gives someone's exact words in quotation marks. Reported speech tells us what they said without repeating the exact words.

Practice example — invented, not a real rival's quotation:
Direct: The player said, “Competition makes me work harder.”
Reported: The player said that competition made them work harder.

X said that... X told an interviewer that... This suggests that... As a result,... However,...

Check the changes: Pronouns change to fit the speaker. After “said”, present verbs often change to past verbs: “makes” → “made”. Use said that or told someone that. A tense may stay the same if the information is still true.

For your project: Find real statements about the rivalry, ideally from both sides. Save the speaker, date, and source. Include direct and reported speech. If a quotation cannot be checked, choose another one. Never turn a summary into a quotation.

🧭 A Clear Report Plan

Cover and contents → Short summary → Introduction → Background → Key events → Positive and negative effects → Statements and reported speech → Conclusion → Source list.

Go beyond a list of facts: Explain why an event matters. Use this pattern: What happened? → What changed? → How does this support your point?

Useful questions: Did the rivalry lead to better ideas or performances? Did it cause stress, unfair behaviour, or conflict? Was it mainly a personal rivalry, a business competition, or a story created by fans and the media? Support your answer with evidence from both sides.

Research tip: Start with 3–4 reliable sources as a useful target, then add more if needed. Use sources such as original interviews, official records, books, and well-known news reports. Record the title, author or organisation, date, and link for each source.
